# Study brief: what makes the website clear, useful and worth revisiting?

All participants below are **synthetic adult audience roles**, not real people,
survey results or statistically representative samples. Simulate contrasting
questions and interpretations. Do not impersonate named teachers, children,
school officials, existing subscribers or actual local businesses.

## Suggested adult roles

- Primary teacher planning a classroom topic before visits are available.
- Secondary teacher checking evidence, curriculum limits and preparation time.
- School excursion coordinator looking for confirmed access and booking facts.
- Parent considering a future family visit and what is currently available.
- Local adult interested in coffee growing and dated development milestones.
- Local grower or maker checking whether a partnership enquiry is appropriate.
- Visitor using a phone who needs a concise reason to register for news.
- Adult with access needs checking whether the copy states confirmed details
  rather than assuming a future site is accessible or already open.

## Copy-ready prediction requirement

Using only the supplied public Glenolia facts and website text, explore how a
small group of synthetic adult teachers, parents, potential visitors and local
community members might discuss the website before the farm is open. Work in
English. Focus on clarity, trust, reasons to return, teacher usefulness, signup
intent, missing information and accidental impressions of promises. Distinguish
what is published, what is planned and what is unknown. Do not invent real
reviews, attendance, partnerships, facilities, curriculum endorsement, harvest,
opening dates or conversion rates. Produce research hypotheses and concrete
copy/design questions to test with real people, not a prediction of actual
sales or a claim that anyone has been surveyed. State the limits of the seed
and the synthetic method. Do not browse or submit forms on the production site.

## Questions to compare

1. Can each role tell what exists now versus what is in preparation?
2. Does the September 2027 target sound appropriately qualified?
3. What would make an adult teacher register before farm visits open?
4. Are the teacher materials and character illustrations understandable without
   implying an endorsement, real farm photo or guaranteed learning outcome?
5. What missing practical information should be confirmed before it is added?
6. What makes the progress pages worth revisiting without hype?
7. Is the newsletter invitation clear about its purpose and frequency?
8. Which mobile wording or route feels confusing, and what should a real browser
   check next? These simulated comments cannot prove a UI defect.

## Starting size and cost

Once John confirms the provider accounts, the actual available allowance and a
spending limit, begin with a small synthetic study (for example 8 adult roles,
3 rounds, one platform if the current UI supports it). This is a starting choice,
not an enforceable token or money cap. Graph extraction, persona setup, reports
and retries also call paid services. Inspect the provider's real usage settings
and the current MiroFish controls before starting. Never launch automatically.

If a creation times out or returns an ambiguous result, inspect saved project,
graph, simulation and provider state before retrying. Do not duplicate a job.

## Report labels

Keep three sections separate:

- **Synthetic audience hypotheses:** what the model generated; unvalidated.
- **Observed website checks:** exact URL, timestamp, viewport, steps, result and
  evidence from actual HTTP/browser work.
- **Owner decisions or open questions:** facts or offers requiring confirmation.

Do not turn a simulated post, invented quote or role's opinion into a real
testimonial, verified defect, demand forecast, curriculum endorsement or metric.
